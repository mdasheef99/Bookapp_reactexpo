My recommendation is to proceed with the Library direction, but revise the capture model before generating screens. The largest problem is not OCR placement: it is the relationship between an exact passage, a reader’s own note, and two save operations with different constraints. A polished capture wizard will still confuse readers if that relationship remains unresolved.

I treat the implementation and verification details you supplied as the factual baseline, not as independently verified findings. Everything recommended below is a proposal. Clubs is not a specification for this review.

1. Prioritized critique

Priority	Assessment	Consequence
Critical	Passage text and personal interpretation need distinct identities.	Do not let a reader unknowingly edit their interpretation into something later displayed as a quotation.
Critical	Save constraints currently threaten to appear too late.	A reader should know before capturing that a saved passage requires a page and has both word and character limits.
Critical	“Bounded full-text review” is misleading during overflow.	The review contains the available extracted text, which may be incomplete. Explicit acceptance does not restore omitted text.
High	Mandatory preparation, selection, metadata and preview stages create unnecessary ceremony.	Make preparation optional and combine passage editing, page entry and exact save preview.
High	Two equally prominent Book Details creation buttons would overstate creation relative to browsing and reading-status management.	Make capture the primary creation action, not automatically the dominant action on the entire page.
High	Passage replacement is a first-class journey, not a generic confirmation.	Show what will be replaced, preserve the proposed text on cancellation/failure, and prevent duplicate submissions.
Medium	The Shelf’s material language is unsuitable as an unchanged template for editors.	Preserve its warmth through typography, color and spacing; reduce texture and ornament where readers work with text.

The strongest elements are book-first capture, explicit saving, no automatic rewriting, temporary photos, and retaining a route for writing a note without scanning anything. These create a credible product boundary.

The weak point in the proposed flow is “source/page/category.” The book is already selected, page is mandatory only for passages, and categories belong to notes under the supplied constraints. Combining these into one generic metadata step obscures the distinction instead of simplifying it.

Challenge to CTA placement: On Book Details, place a visible Capture passage button in a “Notes & passages” section near the reading controls, rather than below a long metadata block. Put Add note beside it as a lower-emphasis, clearly labeled action. On the Notes view, reverse that emphasis: Add note is primary and Capture passage secondary. On the Passages view, capture is primary.

This is context-sensitive hierarchy, not an inconsistent design system. A reader arriving to write their thoughts should not have to visually negotiate around the scanner.

Defer Home quick capture. Book-first entry removes source ambiguity and avoids adding book selection to the immediate capture task. Revisit it only if reader testing shows that returning to Book Details is a substantial obstacle.

2. Information architecture: one place to find material, two explicit record types

Minimal alternatives:

Alternative	Benefit	Cost	Recommendation
Separate Notes and Saved passages destinations	Closest to current implementation	Readers must remember where something was saved; empty states and actions fragment	Acceptable fallback, weaker long-term model
One book-level destination with Notes and Passages views	Shared retrieval location with honest distinctions	Requires clear navigation and record labels	Choose this
One mixed chronological collection by default	Everything appears together	Mixed constraints, actions and overlapping Quote notes/passages become harder to understand	Defer until retrieval behavior justifies it

Recommended structure:

Library: Owned / Wishlist, with List / Shelf as presentation choices. Preserve existing filtering, status management and Shelf behavior.
Catalogue search: A distinct destination labeled Search catalogue. Do not label it simply “Search” in a context where it could plausibly search saved writing.
Book Details: Reading status and book information, creation actions, then a “Notes & passages” preview.
Notes & passages: One book-level destination with two accessible views: Notes and Passages.
Passage detail: Saved text, known attribution, page, and explicit available actions.
Note editor: Personal writing with the existing category model and optional page.

This is a presentation and navigation proposal, not a request to merge records or change schemas.

Use Passages, not “Saved pages” or “Vault.” “Saved pages” suggests a retained page image or complete page, neither of which is promised. Explain once near image capture: “Only the text you save is kept. Photos are temporary.”

The distinction cannot honestly be “notes are always your thoughts” because Quote is an existing note category. Explain it operationally:

Passage: Text from the book, saved against a required page.
Note: A quote or your own writing, with an optional page and a note category.

That overlap is real. Labels cannot eliminate it. Existing Quote notes should remain notes; do not silently reclassify or duplicate them.

Keep Quote / Reflect / Distill / Apply available in the note editor with brief explanations. In particular, Distill should clearly mean the reader writes their own concise takeaway, not that the app generates one.

3. The shortest useful capture/save flow

For a new passage:

Book Details → source choice → review/edit and page → Save passage → passage detail.

Photo preparation is an optional branch within that journey.

Surface	Recommended content and behavior
Source sheet	Take photo, Choose photo, Paste text. Show relevant capability limitations and a short “Page required · up to 800 words and 4,000 characters” explanation.
Image preview/preparation screen	Full-screen image, Use photo, Crop, Rotate, Reset, and retake/choose another. A good image should require no crop gesture.
Extraction state	Remain in the capture context, show indeterminate progress and Cancel. Do not invent percentage progress or promise completion based on the sign-test timings.
Review screen	Available extracted text, passage editing, live limits, required page, known book attribution, and Save passage. This edited text is the exact save preview.
Replacement confirmation	Only when an existing passage occupies the same book/page. Show existing and proposed content clearly.
Passage detail	The actual saved result and clear return navigation. Offer Add note as a separate next action.

Paste bypasses image preparation and OCR. It must not display an extraction animation merely to make the paths appear consistent.

Avoid a compulsory separate “preview” screen when the review editor already shows exactly what will be stored. Before submission, its persistent summary should identify the operation, book and page. Saving should not trim or transform the text behind that preview.

For adding personal interpretation after capture, offer Add note from passage detail, carrying the known book and page into a separate note operation. Do not imply that the note is structurally attached to that passage unless existing behavior supports such a relationship.

Mobile selection

Do not build the entire interaction around dragging two handles across a long OCR result.

For a short result within limits, start with all available text in the editable passage field.
For a long result, make Use selected text explicit. Never silently choose the first words.
Allow native text selection, but also support ordinary editing/deletion and an accessible way to select a contiguous range, such as choosing a start and end paragraph.
Let readers refine the resulting text at character level.
Avoid discontiguous selection in U2. Joining separated fragments introduces quotation-integrity questions that this release does not need.
Keep the original photo available temporarily for comparison while the capture session exists. Otherwise “check the exact passage” becomes a memory task.

If OCR output does not preserve useful paragraphs, the alternative-selection design needs testing against real output before approval. A visual prototype cannot prove that it works.

Keyboard, large text and assistive use

The review is a full screen because text editing, selection and error correction need space. A keyboard-obscured bottom sheet is a poor primary workspace.

Use a keyboard-aware layout; let the editor and page field scroll into view. At large text sizes, stack the save summary and actions rather than compressing them. Ensure TalkBack can read the text, operate alternatives to selection gestures, discover limit errors and reach Save in a predictable order. Do not announce changing counters on every keystroke.

Source choices fit in a sheet. A long old/new replacement comparison should expand to a full-screen surface rather than become two tiny scrolling panes.

Do two save destinations confuse readers? Yes, if presented as a late “Where should this go?” question.

Determine the operation from entry:

Capture passage starts a passage operation.
Add note starts a note operation.
Scan page inside the note editor supplies text to that note; it must not unexpectedly save a passage.

Where changing type is needed, use an explicit Use text in a note instead action. Show the changed limits, preserve the text, and save nothing during the switch. Never offer “Save both” as an implicit convenience.

For scanning into a nonempty note, confirm whether recognized text will be inserted at the cursor or replace existing text. Cancellation must preserve the note.

4. Required state matrix

These are proposed behaviors, not claims about current implementation.

State	Required presentation and recovery
Camera permission denied	Explain that camera access is unavailable; retain Choose photo and Paste. Offer settings only when appropriate to the actual permission state.
Gallery access denied or restricted	Respect the platform picker’s capability. Explain the observed restriction without demanding broader access unnecessarily; retain other sources.
Camera/gallery canceled	Return to the prior capture/editor state without an error or lost text.
Unsupported platform	Expose only working image-extraction capabilities. Keep text entry/paste where supported. Web and iOS need their own capability and accessibility treatment.
Unsupported script	Explain that current local image recognition supports English Latin text. Do not imply multilingual support or confuse this limitation with a ban on manually entered text in other scripts.
Extracting	Indeterminate progress, cancel, no fabricated estimate. Prevent overlapping extraction requests.
Extraction canceled	Return to the image/text state; disregard late completion. Do not erase previous valid text.
No text found	Say no text was found in this image; offer rotate/crop, another photo or paste. Do not open a blank “successful” result.
Rotated or difficult image	Make rotation, crop and retake discoverable. Preserve useful existing text until a new result is deliberately adopted. Do not invent OCR confidence scores.
Review exceeds 20,000 Unicode code points	Persistent message that some extracted text is missing. Require explicit acceptance before either note or passage saving. Editing resets acceptance, as specified. Retake a smaller section remains available.
Note over 500 whitespace-delimited words	Show the count and corrective guidance; block note save without truncation.
Passage over 800 words or 4,000 characters	Show both counters; identify either exceeded limit. Both must pass. Confirm the backend’s character-count definition before implementing the counter.
Passage page missing or invalid	Visible required field; inline error and focus on attempted save. Require a positive page; never infer it from the image.
Existing same-book/page passage	“A passage is already saved for this page.” Show old/new text and book/page; Replace passage or Keep existing. Canceling preserves proposed text for further work.
Network failure during save	Keep text and metadata; explain that save is unconfirmed/failed according to what is actually known. Retry must not create duplicates.
Ambiguous save timeout	Do not treat it as proof that nothing saved. Reconcile the existing result where supported before resubmitting; define this behavior before claiming duplicate safety.
Save in progress / duplicate taps	One active submission, labeled progress, guarded duplicate activation. Keep entered work available until the outcome is known.
Unsaved exit	For meaningful unsaved changes: Keep editing / Discard changes. Cover Android Back, navigation and dismissal where controllable. This does not protect against process death.
Cloud disabled	No enabled “Upload,” “Improve with cloud,” or misleading retry promise. Recovery choices remain local correction, another image and paste.
Passage detail loading/failure	Show loading, then either the saved record or a recoverable error. Never display the unsaved candidate as proof of persistence.
Passage deletion	Clearly identify the record and consequence. Remove it from the list only according to confirmed behavior; provide recovery if deletion fails.

Future cloud retry should remain a separate, per-image consent action that preserves prior valid text on refusal or failure. Design does not need to expose that deferred service now.

5. Must design now, later, and unnecessary

Scope	Recommendation
Must design now	Book-level IA; contextual creation actions; photo/paste entry; optional preparation; extraction states; accessible review/selection; distinct note/passage rules; overflow acceptance; page validation; overwrite comparison; save failure/duplicate handling; passage detail/edit/delete; unsaved-exit behavior.
Must specify now, verify before release	Android Back and keyboard behavior; assistive alternatives; platform capability differences; text-count parity; cancellation and retry behavior; temporary-photo cleanup; real-device OCR/resource acceptance.
Later	U4 personal capture search and attributed plain-text/Markdown export; U5 quiet resurfacing; optional Home capture; multilingual/cloud capabilities if separately authorized.
Not needed for this release	Mandatory crop, mandatory category for passages, a preview screen duplicating the editor, a new app-level tab, mixed-feed complexity, OCR confidence decoration, engagement counts or gamification.

U3 draft recovery is useful but not the next design priority. OCR correction can represent substantial effort, and interruption is plausible on mobile. However, recovery introduces account isolation, expiry, restoration choices and incomplete-session semantics. Those are real product obligations.

My recommendation is to defer U3 provisionally, implement a discard warning as the minimum, and test interruption loss before prioritizing full recovery. This is a proposed change to the previously approved plan—not an accepted scope change.

A discard warning only covers deliberate exits. It does not prevent loss when Android terminates the app. If that loss is frequent during real capture sessions, U3 should move ahead of personal search and export.

If recovery proceeds, restored text-only work must explicitly say that the original photo is no longer available. Do not show a misleading image placeholder or a nonfunctional “compare photo” action.

6. A visual system that belongs to Shelf without imitating it

Shelf can remain the expressive browsing surface. Text work should inherit its palette and restraint, not its literal materials.

Use warm, quiet surfaces with strong text contrast. Keep wood, cloth and brass treatment primarily in Shelf and limited framing.
Use typography to distinguish book text, reader writing and interface labels. A restrained serif can suit passages, but must remain legible at small sizes and enlarged settings.
Avoid textures behind editable or selectable text, faux paper edges around errors, and ornamental controls that resemble decoration.
Give passages comfortable line spacing and paragraph separation. Preserve meaningful source formatting rather than automatically “cleaning up” prose.
Make controls recognizable through labels and boundaries, not brass color alone.
Retain a straightforward List alternative and explicit controls wherever Shelf gestures or arrangement interactions are involved.
Use motion sparingly; retain reduced-motion behavior across capture transitions as well as Shelf.

Prototype records should be clearly identified as sample content. Do not fabricate OCR accuracy, saved counts, timestamps, reading activity or unknown attribution to make a screen look complete.

I would use four as the main review lenses, with Designing Interfaces as a supporting pattern reference. These are applications of the books’ ideas, not claims that the authors prescribe this particular product design.

Book	Useful concept	Application and limitation
The Design of Everyday Things — Don Norman	Conceptual models, signifiers, feedback and constraints	Readers should understand whether they are saving book text or a note, what Save will do, and what incomplete extraction means. A warning must explain the consequence, not merely signal trouble.

Remaining questions

Does the existing same-book/page rule effectively allow only one saved passage per page? If so, that limitation must be explained before readers try capturing a second excerpt.
Does “positive page” mean a positive integer only, and how should unnumbered pages, Roman numerals or ebooks be handled under the current backend?
What does the backend count as a character for the 4,000-character limit?
Does an existing note support a passage relationship, or only shared book/page context? The design must not imply a relationship that is absent.
What currently happens when scanning into a nonempty note?
Is U3 deferral accepted, with the understood risk of losing work after process termination?

These questions affect behavior and can be resolved without new schema decisions. They should be settled in the design brief rather than answered accidentally by generated screens.
